Hortatory Exposition Knowledge Codex

Class 12 English Master Guide

Purpose of Hortatory Exposition

A Hortatory Exposition text aims to persuade the reader/listener that something should or should not be done. Unlike Analytical Exposition (which only proves a thesis is true), Hortatory texts always conclude with a concrete call to action or recommendation.

The 3 Structural Pillars

1. THESIS

Introduces the controversial issue or topic and clearly states the author's official stance/position.

2. ARGUMENTS

Provides logical reasons, research data, expert evidence, or facts to back up the author's stance.

3. RECOMMENDATION

Presents stronger recommendations/solutions regarding what should / should not happen or be done.

Key Linguistic Features

  • Modal Verbs: should, ought to, must, need to (indicates urgency or recommendation).
  • Evaluative Language: critical, crucial, harmful, beneficial, alarming (adds judgment).
  • Connectives / Transitions: firstly, furthermore, however, therefore, in conclusion.
  • Simple Present Tense: Stating current facts and general truths.
